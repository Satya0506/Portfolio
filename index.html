& Re-engagement Strategy

Diagnosed the drivers behind declining Monthly Active Users for a major digital payments platform and translated the findings into a targeted re-engagement strategy that delivered a 5% increase in MAU.

01

Identify the Churn Pattern

Analyzed six months of customer activity across key payment categories to establish the MAU trend, identify when the decline began, and define the customer cohort driving the change.

02

Investigate Churn Drivers

Evaluated multiple hypotheses-including service complaints, promotional withdrawal, competitive offers, and prolonged inactivity-to determine which factors showed meaningful association with churn.

03

Isolate the Competitive Signal

Compared churned, complaint, dormant, and competitor-associated customer segments. Approximately 40% of the churned base overlapped with the competitor base, identifying competitive offers as a significant churn driver.

04

Validate Through Customer Research

Selected 10% of the churned population for customer research conducted with Operations. Survey findings independently reinforced the analytical evidence, with stronger competitor offers emerging as a key reason for disengagement.

05

Design a Controlled Re-engagement Strategy

Partnered with Product Managers across payment categories to align available promotional offers and budgets with the churned population, then coordinated a four-week campaign while isolating the cohort from unrelated promotions.

06

Measure Business Impact

Owned in-campaign and post-campaign analysis, including lift measurement. The four-week evaluation window reduced distortion from novelty and normal beginning/end-of-month usage patterns, contributing to a 5% increase in MAU.

03 · FINTECH · CUSTOMER ENGAGEMENT

Push Notification

Optimized promotional targeting for a major digital payments platform after identifying redundant cross-category outreach and inefficient targeting of dormant customers-reducing monthly push volume by approximately 50% without materially impacting engagement across key transaction categories.

01

Identify Push Overload

Analyzed promotional outreach after monthly push volume reached approximately 1 billion. Independent category campaigns were targeting customers without cross-category coordination, resulting in some users receiving multiple promotional messages on the same day.

02

Isolate Redundant Spend

Identified dormant customers-users with no app activity or transactions for more than 90 days-who were already covered by dedicated re-engagement campaigns but continued to receive category-level promotions, creating redundant outreach and unnecessary third-party messaging costs.

03

Design the A/B Experiment

Designed an experiment that excluded the dormant population from regular category promotions while retaining the existing re-engagement strategy, testing whether communication volume could be reduced without compromising category-level engagement.

04

Protect Key Categories

Monitored performance daily for two weeks, with particular attention to high-performing categories such as UPI, electricity payments, and mobile recharge. A rollback plan was established to immediately restore the dormant audience if engagement deteriorated.

05

Validate the Hypothesis

Observed no material decline in engagement across key transaction categories during the two-week test, demonstrating that the additional category-level outreach to dormant customers was not generating meaningful incremental engagement.

06

Scale the Optimization

Removed the dormant population from regular category promotions. In the following month, overall push volume decreased by approximately 50%, reducing customer over-messaging and third-party communication spend while maintaining engagement across key categories.

04 · BANKING · CUSTOMER 360

Customer Identity Resolution
& Data Quality at Scale

Evaluated a new IBM MDM customer-matching engine for a major North American bank across 700M+ customer records and seven lines of business, providing the evidence needed to compare it against the legacy engine while uncovering upstream data-quality issues limiting identity resolution.

01

Frame the Identity Problem

The bank’s legacy seven-rule matching engine linked customer records using combinations of attributes such as name, SSN, address, and ZIP code. It was producing both over-consolidation-different customers assigned to one identity-and under-consolidation-the same customer represented by multiple identities across products.

02

Benchmark the New Matching Engine

Compared the legacy engine with an IBM MDM engine using approximately 44 matching rules. Evaluated both approaches across more than 700M customer records spanning seven lines of business to determine whether the new engine reduced identity-resolution errors sufficiently for business sign-off.

03

Automate Validation Under Tight Timelines

Built automated SAS SQL validation scripts-the platform available within the vendor environment-to execute rule-based comparisons at scale. Completed the core algorithm assessment in under one month despite stringent delivery timelines and concluded that the new engine materially improved customer matching.

04

Discover the Upstream Constraint

Extended the investigation beyond the original algorithm-comparison scope after finding that even stronger matching rules could fail when source customer data was incomplete, inconsistent, inaccurate, invalid, or stale.

05

Build LOB-Level Data Quality Reporting

Produced data-quality reports for each line of business across five dimensions: accuracy, completeness, consistency, validity, and timeliness. Traced identity failures back to source-data patterns so LOB leaders could distinguish algorithm limitations from upstream data defects.

06

Turn Findings into Data Quality Action

Shared LOB-specific findings with business leaders and recommended data-quality checkpoints within upstream ETL pipelines before records reached the enterprise golden source. The additional value uncovered beyond the original scope helped expand the engagement from two months to eight months.
